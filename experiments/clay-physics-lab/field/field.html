QLOBE KIDS · STORED SDF + COLOUR FIELD STUDY

One lump of clay, no pieces in it

Tap the table to drop a ball; drag the clay to pull it. Every ball you add is smooth-unioned into a single 80³ grid of distance and colour and is never stored again. Pull through a colour boundary and the colour comes with the material, because there is nothing else for it to belong to.

Representation: one 80³ RGBA8 volume — colour in RGB, signed distance in alpha over ±4 voxels — raymarched with hardware trilinear filtering. A pull is semi-Lagrangian advection of the stored field inside a brush falloff, followed by a bounded Lipschitz repair and a local volume renormalisation. Nothing anywhere in this page holds a list of the balls you dropped.

What this page answers

It answers whether pulling material as stored-field advection reads as clay at interactive rates, and whether raymarch cost stays flat as a creature gets complicated — run CLAY_FIELD_LAB.costCurve() in the console for that measurement. It cannot answer whether this survives an old iPad, whether the material passes art review against the implicit-lobe study, or whether a child prefers clay that cannot be taken apart again. The incumbent engine’s own local-deformation comparison is the plastic PBD study.
